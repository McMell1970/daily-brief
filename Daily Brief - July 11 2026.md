**b) Concept of the day: Bollinger Bands (20,2)**

Bollinger Bands answer a deceptively simple question: is today's price *unusually* high or low, given how this instrument has been behaving lately? The construction: take the 20-day moving average, measure the standard deviation of the last 20 closes (how wildly price has been swinging), and draw lines 2 deviations above and below. When volatility rises the bands widen; when it falls they squeeze — so the bands adapt to each stock's own temperament, which fixed percentage bands can't do. Roughly 90-95% of closes should fall inside them, so a close outside the band is a statistically rare event: near the lower band (like your memory names at 28-41% this week) suggests stretched-cheap within the current regime; hugging the upper band (like the S&P at 91%) suggests stretched-rich. They work best in range-bound markets as mean-reversion guides, and fail exactly when it matters most: in a powerful trend, price can "walk the band" — riding the upper band for weeks while every touch looks like a sell signal that never pays. Rule of thumb: bands tell you price is unusual, never *why* — pair them with momentum (RSI, MACD) before acting.
