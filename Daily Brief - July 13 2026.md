**b) Concept of the day: gap risk — why technical levels fail between sessions**

A price gap is the empty space between one session's close and the next session's first trade, created when news arrives while the market is shut. Support levels, stops, and limit orders all assume price moves continuously through them — a gap breaks that assumption: price doesn't travel from $583 to $545, it teleports, and every order resting in between executes at the far side or not at all. This is why a limit buy at $575 "protecting" you is an illusion on a gap-down: you'd be filled instantly at $575 while the market trades $545 — the limit caps what you pay, not what it's worth. It's also why stop-losses are best defined on closes (as your WDC $526 rule is): an intraday or opening gap can spike through a level and recover, and a close-based rule filters that noise. Gap risk works against you most in concentrated, high-beta, globally-traded themes — like memory stocks reacting to a Korean session overnight — and it can't be hedged with orders, only with position size, diversification, and cash. When it fails as a concern: for large-cap diversified indices, overnight gaps are usually small; the concept matters in proportion to how correlated and extended your holdings are. Yours were both, which is why today hurt before New York even opened.
