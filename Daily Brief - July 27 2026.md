**b) Concept of the day — How far price sits above its moving average, and why extension matters:** The "% to 200-day MA" column measures how stretched a stock is relative to its own long-term trend — a reading like MU's +83% means the price is currently 83% above the average price of the last ~250 trading days, which is an extraordinary extension by historical standards for almost any large-cap stock. The intuition: moving averages smooth out noise to reveal the underlying trend, and the further price strays from that trend, the more "borrowed" the recent gains tend to be — extended stocks are statistically more prone to sharp mean-reverting pullbacks, even when the underlying fundamental story (like the AI-memory supercycle behind MU/SNDK) remains genuinely intact. Where this works: it's a useful early-warning that a name has moved a long way, fast, and that normal-sized pullbacks (like last week's chip selloff) shouldn't be mistaken for a trend break. Where it fails: extension alone says nothing about *timing* — a stock can stay extremely stretched for months in a genuine structural bull market (memory chips in 2026 being a live example), so using "% to 200MA" as a standalone sell signal would have meant exiting MU and SNDK far too early. It's best read alongside RSI and MACD, not in isolation.
