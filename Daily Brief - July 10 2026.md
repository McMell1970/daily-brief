**b) Concept of the day: volume confirmation**

Volume tells you how many people backed a price move with actual money, and it's the difference between a vote and an opinion poll. The idea: a genuine trend change should attract *increasing* participation — big up days on above-average volume mean institutions are accumulating; the same candle on thin volume can be short-covering or a handful of eager retail orders that evaporate the next morning. It's measured simply — today's shares traded versus a 20-day average — with no formula subtleties to hide behind. It works best at suspected turning points, exactly like this week: yesterday's +5-8% reversal *looked* decisive, but two of your three names did it on two-thirds of normal volume, and this morning they're giving a third of it back. It fails as a signal in quiet summer weeks, around holidays, and on index-rebalance days, when volume is distorted for reasons that have nothing to do with conviction — and it says nothing about *direction* on its own. Rule of thumb: volume doesn't generate signals, it grades them.
