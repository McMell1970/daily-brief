**b) Concept of the day — Divergence between price and RSI:** A "bullish divergence" occurs when price makes a new low but RSI does not (i.e., momentum is fading less than price suggests) — often read as an early sign that selling pressure is weakening even before price confirms a turn. A "bearish divergence" is the mirror image: price makes a new high while RSI fails to, suggesting the rally is running out of steam. It's a useful early-warning tool because momentum often shifts before price does, but it's also one of the more overused and misapplied signals in retail technical analysis — divergences can persist for weeks without resolving, and using them as a standalone entry/exit trigger (rather than as one input alongside trend and volume) is a common way traders get whipsawed. Worth watching on GOOGL and the memory-chip block over the coming week: if RSI starts basing while price keeps probing lower, that's the kind of divergence pattern that would add real weight to the "selloff overdone" thesis currently resting mostly on BlackRock's word alone.
