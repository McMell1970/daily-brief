**b) Concept of the day: the Stochastic Oscillator (14,3,3)**

The stochastic answers one question: where did price close relative to its high-low range over the last 14 sessions? A reading of 90 means "closing near the top of the recent range"; 10 means "near the bottom." The raw line (%K) is smoothed over 3 days, and a second 3-day average (%D) acts as its signal line — a %K cross above %D from below 20, like all three of your names printed today, is the classic "oversold reversal" trigger. It works best in range-bound or correcting markets, because range position mean-reverts. It fails badly in strong trends: in a real downtrend the stochastic can pin below 20 for weeks while price keeps falling ("oversold can stay oversold"), which is exactly why this brief pairs it with MACD confirmation instead of trading it alone.
