**b) Concept of the day — MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence), and why the "crossover" matters more than the label:** MACD is the difference between a stock's 12-day and 26-day exponential moving averages (EMAs), plotted alongside a 9-day EMA of that difference (the "signal line"). When the MACD line crosses above the signal line, that's a bullish crossover; below, bearish. The intuition: EMAs weight recent prices more heavily than simple moving averages, so MACD reacts faster to shifts in short-term momentum than the 50/200-day SMAs in this brief, while still filtering out single-day noise. Where it works well: catching momentum shifts a few days before they show up in slower-moving averages — useful for timing entries/exits around names that are already on your radar for other reasons (valuation, RSI, catalysts). Where it fails: MACD is a lagging indicator built from lagging inputs (moving averages of moving averages), so in genuinely choppy, sideways markets it generates frequent false crossovers — "whipsaws" — that would have cost money if traded mechanically. It's also entirely price-based and blind to news, earnings, or valuation, which is why this brief treats a MACD signal as one input alongside RSI, moving-average distance, and the actual catalysts in section 1 — not as a standalone buy/sell trigger.
