**b) Concept of the day: the relief rally (dead-cat bounce) — telling repair from reflex**

After a panic day, prices almost always bounce, because the most urgent sellers are done and short-term traders cover shorts and bargain-hunt — mechanics, not opinion. The question is never whether a bounce happens but what it's made of: a *reflex* bounce is driven by the absence of sellers (low volume, fading into the close, leaders still weak), while a *repair* begins with committed buying (above-average volume, a close in the top of the range, and the stocks that led the decline — here, SKHY — turning first). Today's pre-market pop (+2.3% to +4.3%) has no volume information yet and the anchor stock is still falling, so it carries almost no evidentiary weight. The practical use: never upgrade your thesis on a bounce that hasn't printed a full session, and judge it at the close, not the open — the same close-based discipline your stops use. Where the concept fails: at true capitulation lows the first bounce IS the repair and waiting costs you the best prices — that's the fee you pay for confirmation, and with CPI at 14:30 it's a fee worth paying today.
