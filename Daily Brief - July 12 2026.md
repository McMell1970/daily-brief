**b) Concept of the day: market breadth**

Breadth measures how many stocks are participating in a move, rather than how far the index travels. The version cited today — the percentage of S&P 500 members above their own 50-day moving average — answers: is this rally a broad advance or a few mega-caps dragging a cap-weighted index? The intuition: an index can hit highs while most of its members decline, because the biggest names dominate the weighting; that divergence (rising index, falling breadth) has historically preceded corrections, because a rally standing on five stocks has five points of failure. Breadth at 63% and *rising*, as now, is the healthy configuration — more soldiers following the generals. It works best as a divergence detector at extremes; it fails as a timing tool, because breadth can stay weak (or strong) for months while the index does the opposite, and it says nothing about *why* participation is changing. Treat it as context that raises or lowers your confidence in trend-following signals — never as a standalone trigger.
